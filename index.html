Finding North · The North artefact

Looking through someone else’s kaleidoscope

Hielke Gase · Minor Next Gen Leadership

Look through it.
What do you see?
Now ask the person next to you.

What it stands for

No two people see the same image. For me, this stands for what people find important. Everyone looks at work and life through their own kaleidoscope and sees something different.

The kaleidoscope reminds me that I cannot know what someone sees unless I ask.

Where this comes from

Five stories from the minor, placed on the compass.

1. Warns, East · the situation

People check in on each other, and it is still the place where I lead people I also know outside work.

2. My family, East · the situation

They taught me to help, to be honest and to be kind.

3. Daniel, West · wisdom

He showed me that a leader fights for his people, but he decided without asking them.

4. Austria, West · wisdom

My mother looked at my fears and asked what would happen if everything went right. In Austria I found out that I get energy from seeing people grow.

5. Groningen, South · self-knowledge

What made the difference was my mother asking how I was doing.

North · What I have decided

Three ways to lead

  1. Michael Burry, The Big Short:

    decides.

  2. Daniel, my old coach:

    decides and explains.

  3. Me:

    asks, decides and explains.

North · What I have decided

I decide to look through someone else’s kaleidoscope before I lead them.

  • At Plus Stavoren, I discuss a problem with my team first, before we choose a solution.
  • With my football team, I ask every player what they think they are good at, before I decide their position.
  • When others know more than I do, I ask them.

Hielke Gase · Minor Next Gen Leadership · NHL Stenden

The idea of using a kaleidoscope came from a teacher. The card and the decision sentence were developed with AI as a thinking partner. The stories, the moments and the choices are mine.
